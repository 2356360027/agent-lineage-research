When Is Reuse Safe?
Separating Semantic Admissibility from Cost in Language Agents

1. Introduction

A language agent may be offered a correctly computed historical result while
facing a different current request. A trustworthy origin does not establish
that the historical result contains the information required by that request.
For example, a distinct-name list can discard entity multiplicities, whereas a
sum together with a nonnull count can retain exactly the information required
for an average. The suitability of those results depends on the task and the
retained information, not their declared prices.

This study asks whether exposing prices changes a language model's explicit
judgment of semantic admissibility, and whether separating that judgment from
cost-based action selection changes the quality-cost trade-off. We distinguish
three observable stages: the applicability label, the selected action, and the
executed result. A wrong action does not by itself identify a changed task
interpretation. Likewise, a model's short explanation is not direct evidence
of its internal reasoning mechanism.

The proposed evaluation holds the current request, historical query, cached
rows, schema constraints, and trusted-source assumptions fixed while varying
only declared price. It contrasts joint judgment/action generation, visible-
price and hidden-price judgments with a shared deterministic controller, an
independent verifier, and classical reuse/recompute policies. The classical
comparison is indispensable: checking whether a query can be answered from a
materialized view is an established problem, not a novelty claim of this work.
Apache Calcite documents view substitution, residual predicates, join and
aggregate rewriting, and the use of schema constraints [1].

Candidate contributions, conditional on completed evidence:
(a) a controlled diagnostic separating price-sensitive labels from decisions;
(b) reproducible formal tasks with executed caches and information-loss witnesses;
(c) an empirical comparison of separation against strong verification and
classical controls, including null results and full execution costs.
No novel SQL rewriting algorithm or guaranteed-safe LLM certificate is claimed.
If classical rules or independent verification fully explain any benefit, the
paper must be an empirical boundary study, not a new-algorithm success story.

2. Problem formulation

Let S be the public schema and constraints, H the historical query, Q the
current query, and c the complete observed cache. Let W(S,H,c) be the set of
legal databases D such that H(D)=c. Provenance guarantees that the current and
historical queries refer to the same immutable snapshot; its contents are not
otherwise revealed. A reusable result requires an allowed transformation T
such that T(c)=Q(D) for every D in W(S,H,c). For the evaluation, T must be
expressible using the disclosed cache-only operations; mere existence without
an allowed implementation is insufficient.

One negative certificate is a pair D1,D2 satisfying the public constraints with
H(D1)=H(D2)=c but Q(D1) != Q(D2). It proves that c cannot uniquely determine the
requested answer under those assumptions. A lack of a found counterexample is
not a proof. Positive controls instead have a justified executable transform
within a declared supported grammar. Multiple-database testing is related to
prior text-to-SQL semantic evaluation [2], but finite testing and formal
sufficiency must not be conflated.

Let a_hat be the model's applicable/inapplicable/unknown label and p the stated
reuse price. The shared controller selects reuse only if a_hat=applicable and
p is strictly below the known-correct recomputation price; otherwise it
recomputes if permitted or abstains. This controller removes stochastic action
encoding but cannot guarantee safety when the applicability model is wrong.
An invariant hidden-price output is a consequence of the interface design,
not evidence that a model learned price invariance.

3. Evaluation plan

Six information-loss families: entity projection, NULL-sensitive aggregates,
scope/time, complete versus truncated groups, join multiplicity, and ties.
Development and confirmation must be separated by structural subtemplate and
database instance. Default 24 development and 96 confirmation bases are a
resource plan, not a power guarantee; final confirmation size and analysis
will be frozen before model outputs are inspected. A base, not a price variant
or repeated sample, is the paired analysis unit; shared templates also require
cluster-aware reporting. Invalid/unknown outputs are retained separately.

E1 compares explicit labels under price intervention. E2 compares actions and
reuse retention. E3 executes selected transforms/read-only queries and measures
actual costs including verification, fallback, and amortized cache creation.
Formal current SQL is public to every comparator in the controlled study.
Results therefore do not establish natural-language interpretation accuracy.
Any BIRD extension is separate, read-only, and restricted to reviewed semantics.

4. Current development status, not paper Results

One new 32-generation local calibration over two bases has finished and been
verified against frozen source. No negative context was falsely accepted at
any price. The joint method missed both cheap sufficient reuse opportunities;
price isolation, independent verification and narrow classical rules each
retained both. These observations motivate stage-separated measurement but do
not establish a price-induced false-acceptance effect or an advantage over
strong baselines. Confirmation, a second model, actual agent execution and
end-to-end cost comparisons have NOT yet been completed.

Subsequent full development matrix (reported separately from that calibration):
480 local generations covered 24 constructed bases, two cache variants and
three price levels with 12 structural templates. Low-price false-applicability
counts on 24 insufficient caches were 13 for joint judgment, 8 for visible-price
judgment and 14 for price-hidden judgment. The high-price counts were 15 and2
for the first two procedures. Thus the sign of the price contrast depended on
the elicitation procedure; hiding price did not improve semantic classification
in these development data. At high price the visible-label procedure also
rejected more sufficient caches (10/24 accepted, versus20/24 at low price).

On the same low-price action cells, unsafe/useful reuse counts were 9/17 for
joint generation, 8/20 for visible-label control, 14/20 for hidden-label control,
13/19 for independent verification and 0/18 for the configured classical
comparator (denominators24 negative and24 positive bases). These findings do
not support safety superiority of the candidate separation procedure. Summing
all prices would partially reward deterministic control for preventing costly
reuse at tie/high prices; that should not be confused with better low-price
semantic judgment. The verifier reproduced each of its144 candidate labels;
an internal anchoring mechanism is not established by this agreement alone.

The classical comparator used upstream Calcite view-rewriting paths plus a
generic bag-projection substitution, with no private transforms or gold rows.
All48 cache contexts were attempted; two negative-case planner timeouts were
retained as unknown. All18 selected cache-only rewrites matched the snapshot
and342 additional finite checks. These are classical execution audits, not
successful transformations generated by a language agent. All development
outputs were sealed and rescored identically before the present report.
Independent confirmation and real agent execution are still required. A
negative result for the candidate method is retained rather than repaired by
selecting favorable tasks or post-hoc price contrasts.

5. Limitations to retain in the eventual manuscript

Small quantized local models, synthetic formal requests, selected templates,
declared prices rather than invoices, and developer-exposed examples restrict
generalization. Supplying correct current SQL simplifies semantic grounding.
Optional human reviews have their recorded timing/independence limitations.
The planned empirical intervention cannot establish that caching necessarily
induces intent drift. No effect sizes or statistical significance are filled
in before the corresponding frozen experiments finish.
